What is phenology?

Phenology is the study of seasonal timing: when the first Bloodroot flowers, when warblers pass through, when spotted salamanders head for vernal pools. Knowing the calendar means knowing when and where to look.

What you can do

🌀

Season Wheel

A 52-week wheel lets you spin through the year and see what is present, peaking or winding down in any week.

🥾

Plan a walk

Species sorted into At Peak Now, Emerging Soon and Just Past, adjusted for how early or late this year's season is running, plus recent iNaturalist sightings nearby.

🔭

Browse every species

Search and filter by group, habitat, conservation status, or what a species is doing right now: flowering, fruiting, migrating or nesting.

🌲

Learn the habitats

Nine Connecticut habitats, from forest interior and vernal pools to meadows and coastal shoreline, and how each changes through the seasons.

🔗

Follow the cascades

Hand-curated chains of events where one species' activity signals the next: Skunk Cabbage, then Wood Frog, then Spotted Salamander.

🌡️

Track the season's pace

Growing degree days show whether spring is running ahead of or behind normal, and weather alerts flag hard freezes and salamander migration nights.

A look inside

Built on citizen science

Seasonal photos and nearby sightings come from iNaturalist, phenology data from the USA National Phenology Network, and weather from The Weather Company. Created as a Master Naturalist final project with the Connecticut Forest & Park Association program.
