What is phenology?

Phenology is the study of seasonal timing: when the first Bloodroot flowers, when warblers pass through, when spotted salamanders head for vernal pools. Knowing the calendar means knowing when and where to look.

What you can do

🌀

Season Wheel

A 52-week wheel lets you spin through the year and see what is present, peaking or winding down in any week.

🥾

Plan a walk

Species sorted into At Peak Now, Emerging Soon and Just Past, adjusted for how early or late this year's season is running, plus recent iNaturalist sightings nearby.

🔭

Browse every species

Search and filter by group, habitat, conservation status, or what a species is doing right now: flowering, fruiting, migrating or nesting.

🌲

Learn the habitats

Nine Connecticut habitats, from forest interior and vernal pools to meadows and coastal shoreline, and how each changes through the seasons.

🔗

Follow the cascades

Hand-curated chains of events where one species' activity signals the next: Skunk Cabbage, then Wood Frog, then Spotted Salamander.

🌡️

Track the season's pace

Growing degree days show whether spring is running ahead of or behind normal, and weather alerts flag hard freezes and salamander migration nights.

A look inside

Works great on iPad

On a tablet the Season screen splits in two: spin the wheel on the left and watch the species list on the right update. Species photos fill the screen, and everything else stays easy to read in a comfortable column.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. CT Phenology is free, with no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create.

Which devices does it run on?

iPhone, iPad and Android phones and tablets. It is coming soon to the App Store and Google Play.

Does it need an internet connection?

The species guide, season wheel, habitats and cascades are built into the app and open without a connection. Seasonal photos, nearby sightings and weather need a connection.

Does it track my location?

Only if you tap “Use GPS” on the Walk screen, and only while the app is open. Your coordinates go straight to the weather and iNaturalist services to find nearby data. We never receive or store them. You can also use the app without sharing your location. See the privacy policy.

Where does the information come from?

Species timing is based on research and citizen-science data, including the USA National Phenology Network and iNaturalist, plus Connecticut field-guide sources. Weather and growing degree days come from The Weather Company.

I found a mistake or have a suggestion.

Please tell us. Include the species name and what looks wrong. Email us and we will take a look.

Built on citizen science

Seasonal photos and nearby sightings come from iNaturalist. Each photo shows its observer’s username, and photographers keep the rights to their images. Phenology data comes from the USA National Phenology Network, and weather from The Weather Company. This app is not endorsed by or affiliated with those organizations. Created as a Master Naturalist final project with the Connecticut Forest & Park Association program.
